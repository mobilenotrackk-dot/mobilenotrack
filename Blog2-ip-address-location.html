How to Find Location from an IP Address – Accuracy Breakdown (2026)

🗓️ August 2026 ⏱️ 5 min read ✍️ Mobile No Track Team

An IP address is like a digital return address for devices connected to the internet. Every time you open a website, send a message, or stream a video, your IP address communicates with servers to deliver data to your device.

Many people wonder: How accurate is IP geolocation? Can an IP address reveal someone's exact street address or identity? This guide breaks down how IP location lookups work and what they can actually tell you.

One-line summary: IP address lookups can accurately reveal the country, region, ISP, and general city level, but CANNOT reveal a personal street address, house number, or individual identity.

What Information an IP Lookup Provides

When you check an IP address using an online lookup tool, the query searches global IP registration databases (such as RIPE, ARIN, APNIC) and commercial geolocation databases.

🌍 Country & State

99%+ accuracy for determining the country and region of connection.

🏙️ City / Area

Approximate city location based on the ISP routing station or node.

🏢 ISP & Organization

Identifies the Internet Service Provider (e.g., Reliance Jio, Airtel, Comcast).

📮 Postal Code

Broad postal zone associated with the regional internet registry center.

IP Location Accuracy Levels

Location Level Accuracy Rate Notes
Country 99% - 100% Nearly always accurate unless a VPN or proxy is used.
State / Region 80% - 90% Accurate for most major telecom providers.
City 50% - 80% Points to the nearest ISP hub, which may be miles away.
Street / House 0% Impossible via public IP lookups due to privacy protection.

Why IP Location Isn't Always Exact

If you check your own IP address and see a neighboring city instead of your exact location, here is why:

  • ISP Routing Nodes: Your internet traffic is routed through your provider's main hub or tower, which might be situated kilometers away from your actual home.
  • Dynamic IP Addresses: Most home internet connections use dynamic IPs that change periodically, reassigned by your ISP.
  • VPNs & Proxies: Using a VPN routes your connection through a different server altogether, masking your real IP with the VPN server's location.
  • Cellular Networks: Mobile data (4G/5G) often routes traffic through central regional gateways, causing the IP location to appear in major metropolitan centers.
